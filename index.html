Unveiling technology · the heart-rate sensor in a smartwatch

Your watch takes your pulse with light.

A green LED on the back of the watch shines into your wrist. With every heartbeat a little more blood squeezes into the vessels under the skin, and blood swallows green light. A sensor next to the LED sees the light dim and recover about once a second, and the watch counts the dips.

Try this

  1. Press “Light in skin”. More blood → less light back. That dip is a heartbeat.
  2. Arm movement past halfway, rhythm 120 steps/min. The watch counts your steps instead.
  3. Tick “Use the accelerometer”. Your real pulse comes back.
  4. Switch the LED to red. The beat almost disappears.

The watch doesn’t feel your pulse: it watches your skin change colour.

Why green?

Haemoglobin absorbs green about 125× more than red, and green stays in the top millimetre of skin, where the tiny arteries are. So the heartbeat stands out and arm movement matters less. Signal-to-noise with your settings:

Darker skin absorbs green too, so less light comes back. The LEDs are low-power visible light: safe for skin.

How the watch finds beats

  1. Filter: keep only wiggles at heart speed (30–210 BPM).
  2. Find peaks: the top of each beat, never counted twice.
  3. Double-check: the strongest rhythm in the spectrum should agree.
  4. Cancel motion: subtract what the accelerometer predicts.
